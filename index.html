📄 Official Study Abstract (GCBME 2026 Camera-Ready)
Final Revised Abstract

Precise temporal integration between handle pulling kinetics and whole-body kinematics is fundamental in rowing biomechanics, because interpreting stroke efficiency depends on phase-locking force to movement landmarks. Land-ergometer studies have established the measurement basis for this: instrumented rowing systems validated for power output against the Concept2 ergometer [1], a systematic review of inertial sensing cataloguing handle-trajectory and stroke-phase measures [2], and motorized test rigs characterizing wind-braked ergometer behavior under controlled loading [3,4]. However, literature and training protocols routinely assume that independent commercial sensors record synchronously on nominal time bases, ignoring packet arrival jitter, effective sampling bottlenecks, and progressive clock drift (~100 ppm, accumulating ~31 ms over 5 min) that introduce artificial phase shifts and circular reasoning between force and motion. To address this gap, the purpose of this study was to conduct a multi-day temporal audit and validate a motion-derived affine synchronization framework. One healthy young participant performed at least 5 minutes of continuous steady-state rowing on each of three consecutive days (N = 1, 295 strokes), using a custom in-line handle loadcell (100 Hz), Novel loadpads (200 Hz) for hand and thumb, and a Noraxon IMU (200 Hz). The loadcell showed regular timestamps (96.17 ± 4.37% exact 10-ms intervals) and a reproducible clock separation of +103.3 ± 1.6 ppm (R² = 0.990), while the Novel hand unit delivered only 72.95 updates/s within its nominal 200-Hz grid. Motion-derived affine mapping achieved sub-millisecond alignment without circular force reliance (median residual lag 0.516 ± 0.177 ms; 95% limits of agreement −1.77 to +1.85 ms), and peak-anchored dissection resolved every motion-defined cycle (242.1 ± 33.6 N peak handle force, 20.05 ± 0.81 SPM). This framework provides sports scientists and coaches with an auditable, sub-millisecond telemetry standard for phase-accurate force–motion feedback during land rowing ergometer training.

Official Publication Figures (Figures 1, 2, and 3)

These figures directly correspond to the instrumentation, clock drift audit, affine synchronization validation, and biomechanical phase extraction presented in the study abstract.

Figure 1. Experimental setup: (A) TISS Sports Biomechanics Laboratory Concept2 ergometer environment with synchronized multi-sensor instrumentation; (B) Custom in-line S-beam handlebar loadcell (100 Hz, rated capacity 75 kg / ~735 N, C3 accuracy) mechanically coupled to drive chain via U-bolt linkage; (C) Novel capacitive loadpads configured on the handle for palmar hand (200 Hz, 150 × 100 mm) and thumb (35 × 25 mm) metrology; (D) Noraxon Ultium Motion 3D IMU (Object 1, native 200 Hz) mounted to handle as kinematic motion reference.
Figure 2. Cross-device temporal characterization and post-synchronization agreement: (A) Superimposed cumulative Noraxon–Loadcell clock drift across Days 1, 2, and 3, demonstrating an extremely consistent hardware clock drift slope of +103.3 ± 1.6 ppm (R² = 0.990), accumulating ~31 ms of temporal separation over 5 minutes; (B) Bland–Altman agreement of post-synchronization residual lags across all 89 evaluation windows, demonstrating sub-millisecond precision with a mean bias of +0.040 ms, median residual lag of 0.516 ± 0.177 ms, and 95% limits of agreement from −1.77 to +1.85 ms.
Figure 3. Multi-modal biomechanical telemetry and cycle extraction fidelity across three consecutive testing days: (A) Ensemble stroke-normalized Catch-to-Catch profiles (0% to 100%) showing in-line Loadcell tensile pulling force (242.1 ± 33.6 N, primary axis) and Novel palmar contact normal force (secondary axis), with Drive (0%–71.5%) and Recovery (71.5%–100%) phases demarcated by the Finish event at 71.5% of the stroke cycle; (B) Multi-day consistency of stroke cycle duration (inter-day mean: 2.994 ± 0.011 s; pooled stroke-to-stroke: 2.997 ± 0.122 s, ~20 SPM); (C) Deterministic force-peak phase location at 17.77 ± 0.67% of cycle, confirming robust phase-lock alignment between handle kinetics and kinematics.
